# Life Journal — Project Overview

## Overview

Life Journal is a storytelling and life-tracking gameplay mod for The Sims 4.

Its purpose is to help players create deeper, more connected stories for their Sims while preserving the freedom of normal sandbox gameplay.

The central idea is:

> You create the Sim. Life Journal helps you tell, play, and remember their story.

Life Journal is not intended to completely control a Sim's life. Instead, it provides tools that allow players to either create their own stories or receive structured storytelling guidance through Story Blueprints.

A Sim's story should develop from a combination of player decisions, gameplay behavior, relationships, successes, setbacks, and major life experiences.

---

# Core Player Experience

Life Journal supports two primary styles of play.

## Free Journal

Free Journal is intended for players who already have their own story in mind.

The player creates the story while Life Journal helps record and organize important parts of the Sim's life.

Possible Journal information may include:

- Personal journal entries
- Player-created goals
- Major life events
- Career and education history
- Relationship developments
- Family developments
- Important experiences
- My Circle
- Previous Story Blueprints
- Major decisions
- Life milestones

Free Journal does not require the player to participate in a Story Blueprint.

The philosophy behind this mode is:

> You create the story. Life Journal remembers it.

---

## Story Blueprint Gameplay

Story Blueprints provide structured storytelling for players who want additional direction.

A Blueprint establishes the theme and framework of a story without completely determining how that story will unfold.

Blueprints may contain:

- Chapters
- Dynamic Objectives
- Hiccups
- Hurdles
- Crossroads
- Milestones
- Side Stories
- Household POV events
- Story notifications

Player actions determine how the Blueprint develops.

Two Sims playing the same Blueprint should be capable of experiencing significantly different versions of the story.

---

# Starting Life Journal

The current design begins through normal gameplay.

The player obtains access to Life Journal and selects the Sim who will use it.

The Journal becomes associated with that Sim and begins maintaining their personal history.

The planned starting options are:

### Generate My Story

Life Journal helps the player select an appropriate Story Blueprint based on the Sim and the player's desired themes.

### Choose a Story Blueprint

The player manually selects a Blueprint.

### Free Journal

The player uses Life Journal without activating structured Story Blueprint gameplay.

The exact Sims 4 interaction used to obtain and open the Journal will be determined during technical development.

The original concept involved ordering a physical Journal and having it delivered to the household. This idea should be evaluated for Sims 4 feasibility rather than assumed to be the final implementation.

---

# The Main Character

Story Blueprint gameplay revolves around a designated Main Character.

The Main Character is the Sim currently receiving the primary Blueprint experience.

Main Character is a temporary storytelling role rather than a permanent status.

When a Blueprint ends:

- The Sim keeps their Journal.
- Their previous experiences remain part of their history.
- They may continue using Free Journal.
- Another household member may later become the Main Character.
- The previous Main Character may become part of another Sim's story.

This allows one household to develop multiple interconnected stories over time.

---

# Persistent Life History

A Story Blueprint ending does not mean that a Sim's life story is complete.

Life Journal is intended to preserve meaningful information across different periods of the Sim's life.

For example, a Sim may experience:

Childhood
→ Teen experiences
→ University
→ Early adulthood
→ Career
→ Relationships
→ Parenthood
→ Later adulthood

Important events from earlier periods may eventually influence later stories.

Life Journal should not use a universal "100% story complete" system for a Sim's overall life.

Individual Story Blueprints may conclude, but the Journal continues.

---

# Dynamic Storytelling

Life Journal should respond to the way the player actually plays.

Story progression should not rely entirely on predetermined sequences.

Possible information that may influence story generation includes:

- Current relationships
- Career
- Education
- Skills
- Household situation
- Previous objectives
- Previous decisions
- Important experiences
- My Circle
- Player-selected story themes
- Player-selected boundaries
- Actual gameplay behavior

The exact Sims 4 information available to the mod must be researched during technical development.

---

# Dynamic Objectives

Objectives provide short-term direction within Story Blueprints.

Objectives should not always appear as a complete checklist at the beginning of a story.

Instead, objectives may form chains.

Example:

Meet new Sims
→ Become close to someone
→ Invite that Sim out
→ Continue developing the relationship
→ Consider adding that Sim to My Circle

Another player using the same Blueprint may develop a completely different objective chain based on their gameplay.

Objectives should support the Blueprint's theme rather than becoming unrelated generic tasks.

---

# Hiccups

A Hiccup is a slight problem that can grow into a bigger issue or become a meaningful experience—even a good memory. How the Sim gets through it can strengthen their story.

A Hiccup may lead to a larger problem, growth, a stronger relationship, or a positive experience the Sim can look back on. Resolving it should preserve what the experience contributed to the story rather than simply erase the problem.

Example: A plan falls apart, but spending the evening with a new acquaintance becomes the beginning of an important friendship.

Not every Hiccup becomes a Hurdle. Timing and escalation mechanics remain proposals.

---

# Hurdles

A Hurdle is a severe problem that puts the player at an ultimatum. The choice they make heavily affects the story they are creating and can reshape its direction.

A Hurdle threatens something important and asks the player what the Main Character is willing to protect, sacrifice, or change. Its defining feature is the severity of the problem and the weight of the decision, rather than how long it takes to resolve.

Example: A partner demands that the Sim stop seeing someone in My Circle. Protecting the friendship, prioritizing the relationship, or rejecting the ultimatum can each significantly change the story.

A Hurdle may have multiple responses without becoming a lower-stakes Crossroad. Outcomes should meaningfully reflect the player's choice.

---

# Crossroads

A Crossroad is a less severe, multiple-choice decision that contributes to a shift in the story. It can open different opportunities and matter over time without carrying the immediate stakes of a Hurdle.

Crossroads present multiple reasonable options. Their role is to contribute to direction and opportunity, while Hurdles place the player at a severe story-changing ultimatum. Multiple-choice presentation alone does not make a Hurdle a Crossroad.

Example: Choose whether to spend the evening with a new friend, explore a hobby, or attend a career event. Each option can develop a different part of the Sim's story.

See the [Hiccups, Hurdles, and Crossroads System](systems/Hiccups-Hurdles-Crossroads-System.md) for the approved definitions and draft mechanics.

---

# My Circle

My Circle represents Sims who are particularly important to the Main Character.

A high relationship value alone should not automatically make someone part of My Circle.

The system is intended to represent meaningful story relationships.

Circle members may become involved in:

- Major decisions
- Hurdles
- Crossroads
- Advice
- Side Stories
- Relationship developments
- Major life events

Circle membership may change as the Main Character's life changes.

Someone important early in a Sim's life may not remain important forever.

---

# Side Stories

Side Stories allow important Sims around the Main Character to experience smaller connected story arcs.

These stories should not replace the Main Character's story.

Instead, they provide additional context and allow supporting characters to have motivations, conflicts, and experiences of their own.

Potential Side Stories may involve:

- Friends
- Romantic partners
- Family
- Rivals
- Other Sims in My Circle

---

# Household POV

Household POV allows household members to experience the Main Character's story from their own perspective.

A household member does not automatically need to belong to My Circle.

This system is particularly important for children and teenagers.

For example:

A parent becomes heavily focused on their career.

Their child may receive a Household POV event about the parent's lack of availability.

That experience could later become part of the child's own Life Journal history.

This allows the same event to matter differently to multiple Sims.

---

# Experiences and Memory

Life Journal may eventually record important experiences that can influence future stories.

Example experiences might include:

- Career change
- Breakup
- Academic trouble
- Friendship loss
- Financial struggle
- Relationship conflict
- Parenthood
- Major personal achievement

The system may also remember how the Sim responded to the experience.

For example:

Sim A previously experienced Academic Trouble and responded by asking friends for help.

Years later, Sim B experiences a similar problem.

If Sim A is important to Sim B, Life Journal may recognize Sim A as someone with relevant experience.

Sim B could potentially ask Sim A for advice.

The advice may reflect how Sim A actually handled their own experience.

This creates a possible long-term system:

Experience
→ Memory
→ Advice
→ New Decision

This may eventually allow stories to connect across generations.

---

# Therapy and Reflection

Therapy and Reflection is currently planned as a universal Life Journal system rather than something tied to one specific Story Blueprint.

Possible features include:

- Multiple sessions
- Reflection on Journal experiences
- Different meeting locations
- Session costs
- Payment plans
- Insurance
- Reflection goals

Story Blueprints may interact with Therapy and Reflection, but a Sim should not necessarily require an active Blueprint to use the system.

This system remains a later-stage feature and requires technical research.

---

# Story Generator

The Story Generator is intended to help players who want storytelling guidance but do not know which Blueprint to choose.

Possible inputs include:

- Sim life stage
- Career situation
- Education
- Relationships
- Household situation
- Story themes selected by the player
- Story intensity
- Story boundaries

The Story Generator should recommend or construct an appropriate Blueprint experience.

---

# Story Filters and Boundaries

Story Filters describe content the player wants to explore.

Examples may include:

- Romance
- Friendship
- Career
- University
- Family
- Personal growth
- Drama

Story Boundaries describe content the player does not want included.

For example:

A player may want romance while disabling cheating-related scenarios.

Boundaries should allow players to customize story content without requiring completely separate versions of every Blueprint.

---

# Notifications and Presentation

Life Journal should communicate important story developments without constantly interrupting gameplay.

Different information may require different presentation methods.

Notifications may be used for:

- New objectives
- Objective completion
- Discoveries
- Hiccups
- Hurdles
- Milestones
- Chapter changes
- Story developments

Interactive dialogs may be used when the player needs to make a Crossroad decision.

The Journal itself should contain more detailed story information and history.

# First Story Blueprint — Finding My Lane

Finding My Lane is the first Blueprint being designed for Life Journal.

The story focuses on a Sim trying to discover:

- Who they are
- What they enjoy
- What direction they want their life to take
- Which relationships matter
- Which outside influences they should listen to

Possible themes include:

- Friendship
- Romance and identity
- Career
- Education
- University
- Hobbies
- Personal style
- Social groups
- Personal growth
- Outside influence

---

## Chapter I — Branching Out

Chapter I focuses on **discovery and exploration**.

The Main Character is encouraged to branch out, try new experiences, meet new people, and begin learning what they like and dislike. Life Journal should collect story context without deciding the Sim's identity for the player.

### Discovery Information and Chapter II

Chapter I is about getting to know the Sim through gameplay. Their interactions with people, things, activities, venues, and other experiences develop information that Chapter II uses to generate more direct objectives.

For example, repeatedly interacting with an acquaintance may lead to an invitation objective; repeatedly choosing painting may lead to deeper hobby exploration; returning to a venue may lead to sharing it with a friend. These are possible connections, not a fixed checklist or permanent route assignment.

**Chapter I discovers and learns about the Sim. Chapter II uses those discoveries to give the story more direct objectives.**

See the [Finding My Lane Blueprint Plan](Finding-My-Lane-Blueprint.md) for the dedicated story plan and illustrative connections.

### Opening Phase — Getting to Know Me

Current discovery objectives include:

1. Find Your Favorite Music Genre
2. Make 3 New Acquaintances
3. Discover Your Favorite Social Venue
4. Discover Your Hobbies
5. Find Your Style

These objectives are intended to be small, low-pressure ways to learn more about the Sim.

Chapter I should follow the pattern:

**Explore → Experience → Reflect**

Discoveries are not permanent conclusions. Trying something once does not mean the Sim must like it forever.

### Experience Phase

Later Chapter I objectives should use information gathered during the opening phase.

Examples include:

- Spend more time with new acquaintances to learn which relationships may become important.
- Experience a discovered hobby more deeply before deciding whether to develop it.
- Return to social environments the Sim appeared to enjoy.
- Try a new look in everyday gameplay and decide whether it feels right.
- Use music preferences and other discoveries as personalization variables in future objectives.

The player should be allowed to conclude that a person, hobby, place, or style is not right for their Sim. That is still meaningful self-discovery.

---

## Approved Finding My Lane progression

The next chapter begins automatically when all required objectives for the current chapter path are completed or the chapter's time limit expires, whichever happens first. Each chapter has more required than optional objectives; optional objectives do not block completion-based progression. No Journal reflection or player confirmation is required.

Each objective uses a realistic individual Sim-day deadline. Progress during its allotted time does not trigger an objective-related Hiccup simply because it remains unfinished, and inactivity alone does not trigger one before its deadline. An unmet deadline can introduce a related Hiccup. An overdue required objective stays available within the current chapter. It remains required for completion-based progression, but does not block advancement when the separate chapter timer expires. Its handling after that transition remains undecided.

After Chapter I, present a brief chapter overview and reveal only 1–2 upcoming objectives at a time. Completion or a relevant story event can reveal another objective. Exact chapter durations, objective deadlines, objective counts, and handling of unfinished objectives at chapter-time expiry remain undecided.

See the [approved progression rules in the Finding My Lane plan](Finding-My-Lane-Blueprint.md#approved-chapter-progression-rules).

---

## Chapter II — Choosing My Lane

Chapter II focuses on **choice and emerging direction**.

Rather than giving every player the same fixed objective list, Life Journal should respond to the Main Character's actual gameplay.

### Locked Chapter II Rule

> **Chapter II objectives are dynamically generated from the Sim's interactions, relationships, repeated behaviors, and emerging routes rather than being delivered as a fixed objective list.**

Possible emerging routes may include:

- Friendship / My Circle
- Romance
- Hobby / Passion
- Career
- Education
- Lifestyle
- Personal growth

Routes do not necessarily need to be selected from a menu. They may emerge through gameplay.

For example:

- Repeatedly spending time with one acquaintance may strengthen a Friendship or My Circle route.
- Flirting, dating, or pursuing a specific Sim may strengthen a Romance route.
- Repeatedly returning to an activity may strengthen a Hobby or Passion route.
- Work and career behavior may create a Career route.
- University or school behavior may strengthen an Education route.

Multiple routes may develop at the same time.

Chapter I discoveries should inform Chapter II, but they should **not lock the Sim into an earlier answer**. If the Sim appeared interested in gaming during Chapter I but repeatedly chooses cooking during Chapter II, Life Journal should be capable of responding to that change.

### My Circle Development

The acquaintances met during Chapter I may receive small relationship-focused objectives during Chapter II.

The purpose is to help the player determine whether these Sims actually belong in the Main Character's life.

Possible outcomes include:

- Add someone to My Circle
- Continue getting to know them
- Decide they are not a good fit
- Choose nobody yet

Not choosing someone should not automatically count as failure.

### Chapter II Purpose

By the end of Chapter II, Life Journal should have enough meaningful gameplay context to understand which people, interests, ambitions, and lifestyle choices are becoming important to the Main Character.

These emerging routes become story material for the next chapter.

---

## Chapter III — Outside Noise

Chapter III is where the choices and routes developed during Chapters I and II begin producing stronger story consequences.

The central question becomes:

> **Now that I am beginning to understand what I want, what happens when life and other people push back?**

Possible outside influences include:

- Friends
- Family
- Romantic partners
- Coworkers
- Social groups
- Online or cultural influence

Chapter III should use the Main Character's established routes to generate relevant:

- Hiccups
- Hurdles
- Crossroads
- Side Stories
- Relationship developments
- Competing priorities

For example, a strong friendship route may create friendship conflicts or support moments. A career route may begin competing with relationships or hobbies. Two strong routes may collide and create a Crossroad.

Outside Noise should therefore feel like a continuation of the player's story rather than randomly generated drama.

Later chapters have not yet been finalized.

---

# Current Scope

Life Journal is currently in the design and documentation stage.

Not every feature described in this document is intended for the first playable version.

The project should begin with a smaller functional foundation and expand gradually.

A future development plan should classify systems into categories such as:

- Initial prototype
- Version 1
- Future update
- Experimental
- Requires technical research

---

# Current Architecture Decision

Life Journal is currently being treated as one unified mod.

A previous concept explored separating the Journal/life-tracking systems from Story Blueprint gameplay into two compatible mods.

That decision has intentionally been postponed.

The project should remain unified unless technical development provides a strong reason to reconsider the architecture.

---

# Development Rule

This document describes the intended gameplay experience.

It is not an instruction to immediately implement every feature described here.

Before implementation, each major system should move through:

Concept
→ Design
→ Sims 4 Technical Research
→ Prototype
→ Testing
→ Implementation

When design intent and technical feasibility conflict, the intended player experience should be preserved where possible while the implementation is adapted to The Sims 4s.
