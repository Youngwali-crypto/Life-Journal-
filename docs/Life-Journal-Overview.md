# Life Journal — Project Overview

## Overview

Life Journal is a storytelling and life-tracking gameplay mod for The Sims 4.

Its purpose is to help players create deeper, more connected stories for their Sims while preserving the freedom of normal sandbox gameplay.

The central idea is:

> You create the Sim. Life Journal helps you tell, play, and remember their story.

Life Journal is not intended to completely control a Sim's life. Instead, it provides tools that allow players to either create their own stories or receive structured storytelling guidance through Story Blueprints.

A Sim's story should develop from a combination of player decisions, gameplay behavior, relationships, successes, setbacks, and major life experiences.

---

# Core Player Experience

Life Journal supports two primary styles of play.

## Free Journal

Free Journal is intended for players who already have their own story in mind.

The player creates the story while Life Journal helps record and organize important parts of the Sim's life.

Possible Journal information may include:

- Personal journal entries
- Player-created goals
- Major life events
- Career and education history
- Relationship developments
- Family developments
- Important experiences
- My Circle
- Previous Story Blueprints
- Major decisions
- Life milestones

Free Journal does not require the player to participate in a Story Blueprint.

The philosophy behind this mode is:

> You create the story. Life Journal remembers it.

---

## Story Blueprint Gameplay

Story Blueprints provide structured storytelling for players who want additional direction.

A Blueprint establishes the theme and framework of a story without completely determining how that story will unfold.

Blueprints may contain:

- Chapters
- Dynamic Objectives
- Hiccups
- Hurdles
- Crossroads
- Milestones
- Side Stories
- Household POV events
- Story notifications

Player actions determine how the Blueprint develops.

Two Sims playing the same Blueprint should be capable of experiencing significantly different versions of the story.

---

# Starting Life Journal

The current design begins through normal gameplay.

The player obtains access to Life Journal and selects the Sim who will use it.

The Journal becomes associated with that Sim and begins maintaining their personal history.

The planned starting options are:

### Generate My Story

Life Journal helps the player select an appropriate Story Blueprint based on the Sim and the player's desired themes.

### Choose a Story Blueprint

The player manually selects a Blueprint.

### Free Journal

The player uses Life Journal without activating structured Story Blueprint gameplay.

The exact Sims 4 interaction used to obtain and open the Journal will be determined during technical development.

The original concept involved ordering a physical Journal and having it delivered to the household. This idea should be evaluated for Sims 4 feasibility rather than assumed to be the final implementation.

---

# The Main Character

Story Blueprint gameplay revolves around a designated Main Character.

The Main Character is the Sim currently receiving the primary Blueprint experience.

Main Character is a temporary storytelling role rather than a permanent status.

When a Blueprint ends:

- The Sim keeps their Journal.
- Their previous experiences remain part of their history.
- They may continue using Free Journal.
- Another household member may later become the Main Character.
- The previous Main Character may become part of another Sim's story.

This allows one household to develop multiple interconnected stories over time.

---

# Persistent Life History

A Story Blueprint ending does not mean that a Sim's life story is complete.

Life Journal is intended to preserve meaningful information across different periods of the Sim's life.

For example, a Sim may experience:

Childhood
→ Teen experiences
→ University
→ Early adulthood
→ Career
→ Relationships
→ Parenthood
→ Later adulthood

Important events from earlier periods may eventually influence later stories.

Life Journal should not use a universal "100% story complete" system for a Sim's overall life.

Individual Story Blueprints may conclude, but the Journal continues.

---

# Dynamic Storytelling

Life Journal should respond to the way the player actually plays.

Story progression should not rely entirely on predetermined sequences.

Possible information that may influence story generation includes:

- Current relationships
- Career
- Education
- Skills
- Household situation
- Previous objectives
- Previous decisions
- Important experiences
- My Circle
- Player-selected story themes
- Player-selected boundaries
- Actual gameplay behavior

The exact Sims 4 information available to the mod must be researched during technical development.

---

# Dynamic Objectives

Objectives provide short-term direction within Story Blueprints.

Objectives should not always appear as a complete checklist at the beginning of a story.

Instead, objectives may form chains.

Example:

Meet new Sims
→ Become close to someone
→ Invite that Sim out
→ Continue developing the relationship
→ Consider adding that Sim to My Circle

Another player using the same Blueprint may develop a completely different objective chain based on their gameplay.

Objectives should support the Blueprint's theme rather than becoming unrelated generic tasks.

---

# Hiccups

Hiccups are smaller short-term complications.

They represent situations that require attention but have not yet become major problems.

A Hiccup may provide the player with a limited amount of time to respond.

Example:

## Falling Behind

The Main Character has been spending significant time socializing while neglecting school responsibilities.

The player may receive an objective to catch up.

Resolving the issue may end the Hiccup.

Ignoring serious or repeated Hiccups may contribute to a larger Hurdle.

Not every Hiccup must become a Hurdle.

---

# Hurdles

Hurdles are significant story complications.

They may result from:

- Repeated unresolved Hiccups
- Major player decisions
- Relationship problems
- Story events
- Chapter transitions
- Repeated gameplay patterns

A Hurdle should not normally have one required solution.

Instead, the player should have multiple ways to respond.

The purpose of a Hurdle is not simply to punish the player.

A Hurdle should create a new storytelling direction.

---

# Crossroads

Crossroads are interactive decisions connected to the Main Character's current story.

Crossroads should generally contain multiple reasonable choices.

There should not always be a hidden universally correct answer.

Choices may influence:

- Relationships
- Objectives
- Future Crossroads
- Hiccups
- Hurdles
- Side Stories
- Chapter direction
- Journal history

Example:

## Same Night, Different Plans

The Main Character has an opportunity to attend a networking event.

Their friends want to go clubbing that same night.

Possible choices might include:

- Go with friends
- Attend the networking event alone
- Attempt to do both
- Wait for friends

The important part is not which option is "correct."

The important part is how the decision influences the Sim's developing story.

---

# My Circle

My Circle represents Sims who are particularly important to the Main Character.

A high relationship value alone should not automatically make someone part of My Circle.

The system is intended to represent meaningful story relationships.

Circle members may become involved in:

- Major decisions
- Hurdles
- Crossroads
- Advice
- Side Stories
- Relationship developments
- Major life events

Circle membership may change as the Main Character's life changes.

Someone important early in a Sim's life may not remain important forever.

---

# Side Stories

Side Stories allow important Sims around the Main Character to experience smaller connected story arcs.

These stories should not replace the Main Character's story.

Instead, they provide additional context and allow supporting characters to have motivations, conflicts, and experiences of their own.

Potential Side Stories may involve:

- Friends
- Romantic partners
- Family
- Rivals
- Other Sims in My Circle

---

# Household POV

Household POV allows household members to experience the Main Character's story from their own perspective.

A household member does not automatically need to belong to My Circle.

This system is particularly important for children and teenagers.

For example:

A parent becomes heavily focused on their career.

Their child may receive a Household POV event about the parent's lack of availability.

That experience could later become part of the child's own Life Journal history.

This allows the same event to matter differently to multiple Sims.

---

# Experiences and Memory

Life Journal may eventually record important experiences that can influence future stories.

Example experiences might include:

- Career change
- Breakup
- Academic trouble
- Friendship loss
- Financial struggle
- Relationship conflict
- Parenthood
- Major personal achievement

The system may also remember how the Sim responded to the experience.

For example:

Sim A previously experienced Academic Trouble and responded by asking friends for help.

Years later, Sim B experiences a similar problem.

If Sim A is important to Sim B, Life Journal may recognize Sim A as someone with relevant experience.

Sim B could potentially ask Sim A for advice.

The advice may reflect how Sim A actually handled their own experience.

This creates a possible long-term system:

Experience
→ Memory
→ Advice
→ New Decision

This may eventually allow stories to connect across generations.

---

# Therapy and Reflection

Therapy and Reflection is currently planned as a universal Life Journal system rather than something tied to one specific Story Blueprint.

Possible features include:

- Multiple sessions
- Reflection on Journal experiences
- Different meeting locations
- Session costs
- Payment plans
- Insurance
- Reflection goals

Story Blueprints may interact with Therapy and Reflection, but a Sim should not necessarily require an active Blueprint to use the system.

This system remains a later-stage feature and requires technical research.

---

# Story Generator

The Story Generator is intended to help players who want storytelling guidance but do not know which Blueprint to choose.

Possible inputs include:

- Sim life stage
- Career situation
- Education
- Relationships
- Household situation
- Story themes selected by the player
- Story intensity
- Story boundaries

The Story Generator should recommend or construct an appropriate Blueprint experience.

---

# Story Filters and Boundaries

Story Filters describe content the player wants to explore.

Examples may include:

- Romance
- Friendship
- Career
- University
- Family
- Personal growth
- Drama

Story Boundaries describe content the player does not want included.

For example:

A player may want romance while disabling cheating-related scenarios.

Boundaries should allow players to customize story content without requiring completely separate versions of every Blueprint.

---

# Notifications and Presentation

Life Journal should communicate important story developments without constantly interrupting gameplay.

Different information may require different presentation methods.

Notifications may be used for:

- New objectives
- Objective completion
- Discoveries
- Hiccups
- Hurdles
- Milestones
- Chapter changes
- Story developments

Interactive dialogs may be used when the player needs to make a Crossroad decision.

The Journal itself should contain more detailed story information and history.

---

# First Story Blueprint — Finding My Lane

Finding My Lane is the first Blueprint being designed for Life Journal.

The story focuses on a Sim trying to discover:

- Who they are
- What they enjoy
- What direction they want their life to take
- Which relationships matter
- Which outside influences they should listen to

Possible themes include:

- Friendship
- Romance and identity
- Career
- Education
- University
- Hobbies
- Personal style
- Social groups
- Personal growth
- Outside influence

---

## Chapter I — Branching Out

Chapter I focuses on exploration.

The Main Character begins trying different things and learning more about themselves.

Current objective areas include:

1. Find at least two friends
2. Discover a favorite hobby and reach Level 4
3. Find a career path
4. Choose a major if attending university
5. Invite a Sim out
6. Shop for new clothes or explore a new look
7. Declare My Circle
8. Complete the First Journal Entry

These currently represent objective areas rather than a finalized mandatory sequence.

The objective system may dynamically determine which objectives appear and when they appear.

The First Journal Entry may occur earlier in the chapter and help determine which areas the Main Character initially wants to explore.

---

## Chapter II — Outside Noise

Chapter II responds heavily to what happened during Branching Out.

The Main Character has begun discovering what they want.

Now other influences begin challenging those decisions.

Possible influences include:

- Friends
- Family
- Romantic partners
- Coworkers
- Social groups
- Online or cultural influence

The central question becomes:

> Now that I am beginning to understand what I want, how much influence should everyone else have over my decisions?

Chapter II objectives, Hiccups, Hurdles, Crossroads, and Side Stories should be influenced by Chapter I whenever possible.

Later chapters have not yet been finalized.

---

# Current Scope

Life Journal is currently in the design and documentation stage.

Not every feature described in this document is intended for the first playable version.

The project should begin with a smaller functional foundation and expand gradually.

A future development plan should classify systems into categories such as:

- Initial prototype
- Version 1
- Future update
- Experimental
- Requires technical research

---

# Current Architecture Decision

Life Journal is currently being treated as one unified mod.

A previous concept explored separating the Journal/life-tracking systems from Story Blueprint gameplay into two compatible mods.

That decision has intentionally been postponed.

The project should remain unified unless technical development provides a strong reason to reconsider the architecture.

---

# Development Rule

This document describes the intended gameplay experience.

It is not an instruction to immediately implement every feature described here.

Before implementation, each major system should move through:

Concept
→ Design
→ Sims 4 Technical Research
→ Prototype
→ Testing
→ Implementation

When design intent and technical feasibility conflict, the intended player experience should be preserved where possible while the implementation is adapted to The Sims 4s.
