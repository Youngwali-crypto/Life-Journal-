# Finding My Lane — Blueprint Plan

## Status

Design in progress. The foundation, chapter roles, and progression rules below are approved design directions. Examples illustrate possibilities. Exact objective chains, deadline lengths, chapter durations, and ending conditions still need development and technical research.

## Story foundation

Finding My Lane can serve either a Sim discovering their direction or a Sim reconsidering an established life.

Its purpose is to bring engaging story possibilities into everyday Sims gameplay. Interests, relationships, places, experiences, and choices should help the story evolve, rather than turn the Blueprint into a basic aspiration checklist.

Existing careers, relationships, and interests are relevant starting context. Discovery does not require the Sim to start over.

## Chapter structure

| Chapter | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| I — Branching Out | Get to know the Sim through exploration and gameplay. |
| II — Choosing My Lane | Use the information developed in Chapter I to generate more direct objectives, while continuing to respond to gameplay. |
| III — Outside Noise | Bring pressure and consequences connected to the Sim's developing routes and choices. |

## Approved chapter progression rules

### Required and optional objectives

Each chapter has more required objectives than optional objectives. Exact counts and which objectives fill each role remain to be designed.

The next chapter begins automatically when all required objectives for the Main Character's chapter path are completed, or when the current chapter's time limit expires—whichever happens first. This does not mean completing every possible objective in the Blueprint's pool. Optional objectives can develop relationships, opportunities, and Journal history, but unfinished optional objectives do not block a transition.

The chapter has its own time limit, separate from individual objective deadlines. Chapter-time expiry advances the story even if required objectives remain unfinished. Exact chapter durations are not yet decided.

### Individual objective deadlines

Each objective has its own deadline measured in Sim days, matched to the work it realistically requires.

- A short action, such as inviting a Sim out or visiting a venue, may fit within one Sim day.
- A skill-level goal should allow several days of practice.
- Developing a friendship should allow time for repeated interactions.

These are timing principles, not finalized durations. Progress during the allotted time should not trigger a Hiccup merely because the objective is not yet complete. Inactivity alone does not automatically trigger an objective-related Hiccup before its deadline; this supersedes the earlier one-day idleness proposal.

If the deadline passes and the objective is unfinished, a related Hiccup can enter the story. Making progress and completing the objective are separate: progress within the allotted time is valid, while an unmet deadline can still create a complication.

### Overdue required objectives

A required objective stays available after its deadline passes. The player can finish it while the related Hiccup becomes part of the story.

Missing a deadline does not automatically fail the chapter, skip the objective, or unlock the next chapter. Completing all required objectives advances the chapter early; the chapter's own time limit can also advance it. An overdue required objective stays available within the current chapter. Whether unfinished objectives carry over or change when the chapter ends remains undecided. A Hiccup is a slight problem that can grow or become a meaningful experience—even a good memory; a missed deadline does not automatically become a severe Hurdle.

Repeated consequences, extensions, and impossible objectives still need separate rules.

### Gradual objective reveal after Chapter I

At the start of each later chapter, give the player a brief overview of its focus without exposing its entire objective list or outcomes.

Show only **1–2 upcoming objectives at a time**. Further objectives can be revealed by completing an objective or by a relevant story event. Their selection should continue responding to the Main Character's interactions, discoveries, and choices.

The preview count does not yet define a limit on all active objectives. How upcoming objectives become active, when their deadlines begin, and how overdue objectives remain visible need further design.

### Automatic chapter transition

The next chapter begins automatically on required-objective completion or chapter-time expiry, whichever happens first. A Journal reflection or player confirmation is not required to advance. Any chapter-change notification reports the transition rather than gating it.

The transition uses the Sim's discoveries and story history gathered so far. How unfinished objectives affect the next chapter still needs design.

## Chapter I — Branching Out

### Approved purpose

Chapter I is about discovering things to get to know the Sim. Through their interactions with people, things, activities, venues, and other experiences, Life Journal develops information about them.

These discoveries provide context for the transition into Chapter II. They are not permanent labels or commitments.

### Existing opening discovery objectives

From the current overview:

1. Find Your Favorite Music Genre.
2. Make 3 New Acquaintances.
3. Discover Your Favorite Social Venue.
4. Discover Your Hobbies.
5. Find Your Style.

The chapter follows **Explore → Experience → Reflect**.

### Information developed through gameplay

The story should learn about:

- People the Sim interacts with and relationships they return to.
- Things, activities, and hobbies they explore or repeatedly choose.
- Venues and social environments they visit.
- Emerging interests and preferences.
- Existing life circumstances and changes in their behavior.

This describes design intent, not a verified ability to detect every interaction automatically. Specific observations and any player confirmations need technical research.

## Transition from Chapter I to Chapter II

### Approved direction

**Chapter I discovers and learns about the Sim. Chapter II uses those discoveries to give the story more direct objectives.**

Chapter II objectives should be based on the Sim's actual interactions with people, things, venues, and other experiences. The transition should feel connected to what the player has already done.

Discovery information gives Chapter II direction without permanently locking the Sim into a preference or route. Later behavior can change which objectives become relevant. Multiple routes may develop at the same time.

### Illustrative connections

These are possible objective examples, not a fixed mandatory sequence.

| Chapter I information | Possible Chapter II objective |
| --- | --- |
| The Sim repeatedly talks to one acquaintance. | Invite that Sim out and develop the connection. |
| The Sim keeps choosing painting. | Explore painting further or share it with someone. |
| The Sim regularly visits a particular venue. | Bring a friend there or meet someone with similar interests. |

Completing an objective should create further story possibilities. A developing friendship, for example, may eventually lead to shared interests, a memorable Hiccup, outside influence, or a decision about My Circle.

## Chapter II — Choosing My Lane

Approved direction: objectives are dynamically generated from interactions, relationships, repeated behaviors, and emerging routes, rather than delivered as one fixed checklist.

Potential routes already described in the overview include friendship/My Circle, romance, hobby/passion, career, education, lifestyle, and personal growth.

To develop next: route-specific objective chains, eligibility, alternatives, and the required beats for the selected path. Use the approved progression and gradual reveal rules above.

## Chapter III — Outside Noise

Use established routes and choices to make the story shift meaningful.

Approved system definitions:

- **Hiccup:** A slight problem that can grow into a bigger issue or become a meaningful experience—even a good memory.
- **Hurdle:** A severe problem that puts the player at an ultimatum. Their choice heavily affects the story.
- **Crossroad:** A less severe, multiple-choice decision that contributes to a shift in the story.

To develop next: specific complications, choices, and consequences for each route.

## Branches and possible endings

Multiple endings remain part of the Blueprint design. They should reflect accumulated choices, objective outcomes, relationships, Hiccups, Hurdles, Crossroads, and story paths. Different endings can be legitimate conclusions.

Branch maps, ending names, and ending conditions are not finalized in this plan.

## Remaining design work

- Specific required and optional objectives for each chapter path; required objectives must outnumber optional ones.
- Exact chapter time limits and when chapter timers start.
- How unfinished required objectives and sparse discovery information affect a transition caused by chapter-time expiry.
- Whether unfinished optional objectives carry into the next chapter.
- Objective chains and how discoveries make them eligible.
- How sparse or conflicting discovery information is handled.
- Specific route branches and story complications.
- Exact objective deadlines, timer start points, and event response windows.
- How active, upcoming, and overdue objectives are displayed.
- What happens when a required objective becomes impossible, and whether overdue goals can receive extensions or repeated consequences.
- Possible endings and their narrative conditions.
